Industry Performance Across Market Conditions

A whiteboard scaffold — five linked views on how U.S. industries behave when the market is calm or volatile, rising or falling.

1 · Timeline 2 · Conditions 3 · Heatmap 4 · Network 5 · Comparison Data

The question

How does industry performance differ across different U.S. stock-market conditions? We define four conditions from two signals — the market's daily direction (S&P 500 up/down) and how nervous it is (VIX calm/volatile, split at the median). The five views below move from context → overview → pattern → relationship → deep dive.

1 · Market-Conditions Timeline when

S&P 500 (line, left) and VIX (line, right) over ~10 years. The ribbon beneath colours each trading day by its market condition — establishing when each regime occurred.

2 · What a "condition" looks like overview

Every trading day plotted by S&P return (x) vs VIX (y), coloured by condition. The horizontal line is the VIX median (the Calm/Volatile boundary); the vertical line is 0% return. The shaded band is the typical VIX range (15–20), shown for context only.

3 · Industry performance heatmap pattern

Average daily return (%) for each of the 12 industries across the four conditions. Green = gains, red = losses. This is the first real pattern: which industries hold up when the market turns.

4 · Industry similarity network relationship

Industries linked when their returns are correlated above a threshold. Switch the condition and watch the structure change — a first look at whether industries move together more in crises.

5 · Interactive industry comparison deep dive

Cumulative growth of $1 invested per industry over the full window. Toggle industries to compare defensive vs cyclical trajectories through real episodes.
